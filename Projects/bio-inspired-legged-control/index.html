Robot learning · Adaptive control · Embodied intelligence

Bio-inspired learning and fault-tolerant control for legged robots

Legged robots need coordinated movement and the ability to adapt when their environment or hardware changes. My research brings biological rhythm generation, learning from demonstration and online feedback control together on open robotic platforms, connecting locomotion algorithms with physical robot development.

Resilient locomotion through bio-inspired control

Reinforcement learning modulates central pattern generators to adapt coordinated leg motion. Physical trials on an 18-DoF hexapod have demonstrated continued walking under multiple disabled legs, linking learned adaptation with dependable locomotion under hardware failures.

The open-source SPINDER platform connects mechanical design, electronics, analytic inverse kinematics and hierarchical gait control. Its accessible models and software support reproducible experiments from mechanism development to locomotion policy evaluation.

Current research direction

Learning What a Gait Is

Window-level adversarial inverse reinforcement learning from a single insect walk for hexapod locomotion.

This work investigates how the structure of coordinated walking can be learned from one insect-walking demonstration. By studying motion over short windows, the approach explores how biological movement can guide hexapod gait learning with limited demonstration data. The aim is to connect learning from natural behaviour with practical robot locomotion.

Current research direction

Online fuzzy-neural actor–critic control for hexapod trajectory tracking

A complementary direction develops adaptive trajectory tracking that learns during operation. Fuzzy representations, neural approximation and actor–critic learning are brought together to respond to uncertain robot dynamics and changing operating conditions, connecting learning-based adaptation with feedback control.

From learning methods to useful robot capabilities

These directions connect efficient skill learning, motion coordination and fault tolerance with the needs of robots working in changing environments. Together with modular bipedal hardware development under KFUPM Grant INMR2632, they support a broader programme of adaptable, reproducible legged robotics for research and inspection applications.
