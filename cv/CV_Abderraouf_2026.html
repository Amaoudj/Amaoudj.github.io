Research Profile

My research develops embodied intelligence for robot arms, legged robots and robot teams: systems that learn from limited experience, adapt when their hardware or tasks change, and coordinate safely at industrial scale. Over more than a decade, I have carried out academic and industrial research in Algeria, Denmark and Saudi Arabia, working with partners including Siemens, Mobile Industrial Robots and Enabled Robotics. My work connects robot learning, control and mechatronic design to flexible manufacturing, smart logistics and autonomous inspection.

  • Embodied AI and world models: I develop NeuroAct, a modular robot intelligence architecture that combines embodiment representations, hierarchical policies and persistent memory to support adaptation across bodies and tasks. Its shared model has generalised to robot bodies not encountered during training in simulation. My current work develops multi-agent world models that learn how individual and collective actions shape the environment, alongside vision-language-action policies that anticipate physical interactions and reason about their timing.
  • Robot learning for intelligent manufacturing: As Principal Investigator of KFUPM Grant INMR2403, I established a dual-arm Meca500 skill-learning testbed that is now shared laboratory infrastructure in active use. Decentralised policies with graph attention coordinate the physical arms and extend to larger teams in simulation. This work connects reusable manipulation skills, inter-arm coordination and motion planning to flexible industrial automation.
  • Multi-robot autonomy for smart logistics and environmental monitoring: I develop decentralised path finding and scheduling for warehouse fleets operating with limited communication, in collaboration with Enabled Robotics. These methods coordinate large fleets and handle continuous task arrivals. Safety-filtered multi-drone policies improve gas-source localisation over infotaxis and model-predictive-control baselines, supporting research towards coordinated environmental monitoring.
  • Bio-inspired learning and fault-tolerant control for legged robots: Reinforcement learning coupled with central pattern generators enables a physical 18-DoF hexapod to keep walking despite multiple disabled legs, without falls in repeated hardware trials. I am extending this direction through gait learning from a single insect walk using window-level adversarial inverse reinforcement learning, and online fuzzy-neural actor-critic control for hexapod trajectory tracking. The open-source SPINDER platform supports reproducible locomotion research; as Co-Principal Investigator of KFUPM Grant INMR2632, I also develop modular bipedal hardware and actuation.

Research and Development Projects

Next-Generation Robotic Manipulation and Learning Systems

KFUPM · Ongoing · Postdoctoral Research Fellow

Learning-based manipulation for flexible industrial automation combines reinforcement learning, imitation learning and inter-arm attention. A decentralised graph-attention policy runs on physical Meca500 arms and scales from up to four training arms to ten arms in simulation. Current work develops multi-agent world models for sparse-reward coordination and vision-language-action policies, with the goal of reusing manipulation skills as production tasks change.

Laboratory demonstrations · Multi-arm planning video

NeuroAct: Transferable Robot Intelligence Across Bodies and Tasks

KFUPM · Ongoing · Postdoctoral Research Fellow

A modular robot intelligence architecture combines embodiment representations, hierarchical policies and persistent memory to reduce retraining across hardware and tasks. Its shared model has generalised to unseen simulated robot morphologies, providing a foundation for investigating how learned skills transfer between different bodies.

Bio-Inspired Fault-Tolerant Control for Legged Robots

KFUPM · Ongoing · Postdoctoral Research Fellow

Reinforcement learning modulates central pattern generators to maintain coordinated locomotion as joints and legs fail. A single policy trained with randomised fault injection was deployed directly on a physical 18-DoF hexapod without additional hardware training, sustaining walking without falls across repeated trials with multiple disabled legs. This work advances adaptable locomotion for inspection and field robotics; complementary research integrates terrain perception and gait adaptation for quadrupeds.

SPINDER demonstration · Open platform and simulator

Learning Hexapod Gaits from a Single Insect Walk

KFUPM · Ongoing research

Developing window-level adversarial inverse reinforcement learning to capture gait structure from a single insect walking demonstration. The approach studies coordinated movement over time windows, with the aim of transferring biological locomotion patterns into adaptable hexapod behaviour while reducing the need for hand-designed gait rewards.

Online Fuzzy-Neural Actor-Critic Control for Hexapod Trajectory Tracking

KFUPM · Ongoing research

Developing an adaptive controller that combines fuzzy-neural approximation with online actor-critic learning for hexapod trajectory tracking. The research targets continuous adaptation to uncertain robot dynamics and changing operating conditions, connecting learning-based control to precise, reliable legged motion.

Intelligent Bipedal Robotic Platform

KFUPM · Grant INMR2632 · Ongoing · Co-Principal Investigator

Developing open, modular hardware and actuation for locomotion, balance recovery, jumping and obstacle-negotiation research. Replaceable mechanical, sensing and control modules, standardised interfaces and synchronised logging support reproducible experiments and comparison of classical and learning-based controllers.

Collaborative Robotics for Industry 4.0

KFUPM · Grant INMR2403 · 2023–2025 · Principal Investigator

Led the development of multi-cobot skill learning, coordination and motion planning for flexible production, alongside fleet coordination for storage and transport.
